# Day 53 – Reflection

## What I Learned

Before today's lesson, I knew that using a Wi-Fi password was important, but I didn't realize how many different attacks target wireless networks.

I learned the differences between **WEP, WPA, WPA2, and WPA3**, and why WPA3 is currently the most secure wireless security standard.

I also discovered that attackers don't always need to hack into a network directly. They can create fake Wi-Fi networks, set up rogue access points, or launch deauthentication attacks to trick users or disrupt network access.

---

## Key Takeaway

Wireless networks are convenient, but they also introduce security risks because anyone within range can attempt to connect or intercept wireless communication. Proper encryption, strong authentication, and continuous monitoring are essential to protecting wireless environments.

---

## Personal Reflection

Today's lesson helped me understand why wireless security is an important part of network defense.

As a future SOC analyst, I now know that monitoring wireless activity isn't just about detecting unauthorized devices—it's also about identifying suspicious behavior, investigating wireless attacks, and protecting users from threats that originate over Wi-Fi.
