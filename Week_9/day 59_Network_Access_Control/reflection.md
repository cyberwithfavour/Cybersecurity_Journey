# Day 59 – Reflection

## What I Learned

Today's lesson taught me that securing a network isn't just about protecting it from attackers outside the organization. It's also about making sure that only the **right people and trusted devices** can access it in the first place.

I learned that **Network Access Control (NAC)** acts like a security guard at the entrance of a network. Before anyone is allowed in, both the user and the device must prove they are authorized and meet the organization's security requirements.

One concept that stood out to me was **802.1X authentication**. I discovered that devices don't automatically gain access to a network; they can be required to authenticate through a switch or wireless access point before communication is allowed.

I also learned how **device compliance checks** help organizations ensure that devices have updated antivirus software, security patches, and firewalls before they are trusted on the network.

Finally, I understood why organizations separate **guest networks** from internal corporate networks. Even though visitors may need Internet access, they should never have direct access to sensitive business systems.

---

## Key Takeaway

A secure network doesn't trust every device that connects to it. Access should always be verified, controlled, and continuously monitored.

---

## Personal Reflection

Before today, I assumed that connecting to a company's Wi-Fi was as simple as entering a password. I now understand that enterprise environments use multiple security checks before granting access.

As a future SOC analyst, understanding NAC will help me investigate unauthorized access attempts, identify rogue devices, and understand how organizations enforce security policies before an attack even begins.
