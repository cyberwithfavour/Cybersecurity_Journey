# Day 57 – Reflection

## What I Learned

Today's lesson showed me that cybersecurity is not just about deploying security tools like firewalls or antivirus software. The way a network is designed plays a major role in protecting an organization from cyber threats.

I learned that enterprise networks are divided into different security zones, such as the **External Network**, **DMZ**, and **Internal Network**, each with its own level of trust. This separation helps reduce the impact of attacks by preventing attackers from moving freely across the network.

One concept that stood out to me was **Defense in Depth**. Instead of depending on a single security control, organizations implement multiple layers of protection. If one layer fails, other controls continue to defend the network.

I also learned about **trust boundaries**, which are points where traffic moves between networks with different trust levels. These boundaries are important because they are where organizations apply security controls to inspect and filter traffic.

---

## Key Takeaway

A secure network starts with good architecture. Security controls are most effective when they are part of a well-designed network that separates systems, limits access, and continuously monitors activity.

---

## Personal Reflection

This lesson changed the way I think about cybersecurity. Before today, I focused mostly on individual security technologies. I now understand that the overall design of a network is just as important as the tools protecting it.

As a future SOC analyst, understanding secure network architecture will help me investigate incidents more effectively because I will know how traffic is expected to flow, where attacks are likely to occur, and which systems may be affected during an incident.
