# Reflection – Day 49

Today's lesson introduced me to Network Traffic Analysis and helped me understand how data moves across a network.

Before today, I knew devices communicated over a network, but I didn't really think about what was happening behind the scenes. Learning that every activity on the internet is broken down into packets gave me a new perspective on how communication works.

I also learned about Wireshark and why it is one of the most important tools for SOC analysts. Instead of just seeing that devices communicate, Wireshark allows analysts to capture and inspect the actual packets being exchanged. This makes it possible to investigate suspicious activity and identify potential security threats.

Another concept that stood out to me was the TCP Three-Way Handshake. Understanding how devices establish a reliable connection before exchanging data helped me appreciate why network communication is structured the way it is.

I also learned the difference between HTTP and HTTPS, as well as the role DNS plays in translating domain names into IP addresses. More importantly, I discovered that attackers can abuse DNS traffic for command-and-control communication or data exfiltration, which is why monitoring network traffic is such an important part of a SOC analyst's job.

Today's lesson reminded me that cybersecurity isn't just about protecting computers—it's also about understanding how devices communicate and recognizing when that communication doesn't look normal.

As I continue this journey, I want to become more confident using tools like Wireshark to analyze network traffic and investigate real-world security incidents.
