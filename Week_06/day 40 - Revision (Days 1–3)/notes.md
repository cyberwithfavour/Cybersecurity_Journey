# Day 40 – Revision (Days 1–3)

## Objective

Revise the core foundations of cybersecurity by reviewing what cybersecurity is, the CIA Triad, and common cyber threats.

# DAY 1 – Introduction to Cybersecurity

## What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, systems, applications, and data from cyberattacks, unauthorized access, damage, or theft.

The primary goals are to:

- Protect information
- Maintain privacy
- Ensure business continuity
- Prevent financial loss

---

## Why Cybersecurity Matters

Cybersecurity protects against:

- Data breaches
- Identity theft
- Financial fraud
- Malware
- Ransomware
- Business disruption

As technology grows, cyber threats become more advanced, making cybersecurity an essential part of every organization.

---

## Common Cyber Threats

- Malware
- Phishing
- Ransomware
- Social Engineering
- Insider Threats
- Denial of Service (DoS)

---

## Key Takeaways

- Cybersecurity protects digital assets.
- Everyone has a role in cybersecurity.
- Attackers target people, processes, and technology.

# DAY 2 – CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is the foundation of information security.

---

## Confidentiality

Ensures information is only accessible to authorized users.

Examples:

- Passwords
- Encryption
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Access Control

---

## Integrity

Ensures information remains accurate and unchanged unless modified by authorized users.

Examples:

- Hashing
- Digital Signatures
- File Permissions

---

## Availability

Ensures systems and data are available when needed.

Examples:

- Backups
- Redundancy
- Disaster Recovery
- UPS
- Load Balancing

---

## Why the CIA Triad Matters

Every security control supports one or more parts of the CIA Triad.

Examples:

Encryption → Confidentiality

Hashing → Integrity

Backups → Availability

---

# DAY 3 – Threats, Vulnerabilities & Risk

## Threat

Anything capable of causing harm.

Examples:

- Hackers
- Malware
- Flood
- Fire

---

## Vulnerability

A weakness that can be exploited.

Examples:

- Weak passwords
- Unpatched software
- Misconfigured firewall

---

## Exploit

A method used to take advantage of a vulnerability.

Example:

An attacker exploits an outdated web server to gain unauthorized access.

---

## Risk

The likelihood that a threat will exploit a vulnerability and cause damage.

Risk depends on:

- Threat
- Vulnerability
- Impact

---

## Risk Response Strategies

- Accept
- Mitigate
- Transfer
- Avoid

---

# Quick Comparison

Threat

Potential danger.

Vulnerability

Weakness.

Exploit

Attack method.

Risk

Likelihood and impact of a successful attack.

---

# Revision Summary

Cybersecurity protects systems and information from digital threats.

The CIA Triad forms the foundation of information security:

- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Availability

Threats exploit vulnerabilities through exploits, creating organizational risk.

Managing risk is one of the primary responsibilities of cybersecurity professionals.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Cybersecurity protects people, systems, and data.
- The CIA Triad is the foundation of security.
- Threats exploit vulnerabilities.
- Exploits are attack methods.
- Risk measures the likelihood and impact of an attack.
- Organizations reduce risk through security controls and risk management.
